Flaxseed meal works its quiet magic as the only flour in these vegan cinnamon muffins—no eggs, grains, added oil, flour blends, or stabilizer blends required. Cinnamon and vanilla lend familiar warmth, while the moist, tender yet satisfyingly dense crumb calls to mind a classic bran muffin!

If you have been around my website for a while, you know how much I love working with flaxseed meal as a stand-alone ingredient. It can take the place of both flour and eggs, without the need for other flours or complicated binders. Humble flaxseeds can, all on their own, become delicious bread, waffles, breakfast cookies, chocolate chip cookies, tortillas, and so much more!

This recipe began with a reader’s question: Did I happen to have a recipe similar to a particular brand of muffins made with 100% flax? I had not heard of the brand, so I looked it up. The muffins looked wonderful and used flax as their only flour, but they also relied on eggs. I immediately wanted to know whether I could create an egg-free version.

Several gooey, deflated experiments later, I am thrilled to share the delectable, successful result: Vegan Cinnamon Flaxseed Muffins. A chocolate version is in the works, too. 😉

These muffins are fragrant with cinnamon and vanilla, pleasantly toasty from the flaxseed meal, and filling enough to carry you through the morning. Each muffin has 8 grams of protein and 12 grams of fiber. If you enjoy the hearty comfort of a bran muffin, I think you will love these as much as I do.

Why You’ll Love This Recipe

  • Moist, tender texture: The crumb is dense and satisfying without feeling dry.
  • Warm, familiar flavor: Cinnamon and vanilla complement the naturally toasty flavor of flaxseed.
  • Quick and easy: The dough comes together in one bowl after the dry ingredients are whisked separately.
  • High in protein: Each muffin has 8 grams of protein.
  • High in fiber: Each muffin has 12 grams of fiber.
  • Vegan: Egg-free and dairy-free
  • Gluten-free and grain-free: Flaxseed meal is the only flour. Use certified gluten-free baking powder if needed.
  • Oil-free: Almond butter provides richness without added oil. No melted coconut oil, avocado oil, olive oil or vegetable oil added.
  • Rich in Omega-3. Flaxseed is one of the richest plant-based sources of omega-3 fatty acids available.

Ingredients for the Vegan Flaxseed Muffins

The exact amounts of each ingredient are indicated in the recipe card at the end of the post. Toggle between US Customary (volume) and Metric (weights) for preferred measurement option.

  • Flaxseed meal. Flaxseed meal is the only flour in this recipe, and it also helps bind the muffins without eggs. I recommend golden flaxseed meal for its lighter color and milder flavor, but regular dark flaxseed meal works equally well. For the best texture, process the meal in a blender until it becomes a very fine flour. Do not skip this step. You can also begin with whole flaxseeds; see the substitutions below.
  • Whole psyllium husks. Psyllium gives the egg-free muffins extra structure that flax along often cannot. Be sure to check whether you have whole psyllium husks or psyllium powder. They are used at the same weight, but not at the same volume.
  • Baking powder. Baking powder provides the muffins’ modest rise. Use baking powder—not baking soda—because the two ingredients are not interchangeable. Choose certified gluten-free baking powder if necessary.
  • Ground cinnamon. Cinnamon gives these flax muffins their warm, comforting flavor.
  • Salt. A small amount of salt balances the sweetness and brings the cinnamon, vanilla, and flax flavors into focus.
  • Boiling water. Hot water helps to melted the almond butter and hydrate the ground flaxseed and psyllium. Use the specified amount: the mixture needs to be a thick, moist dough rather than a pourable batter.
  • Almond butter. Almond butter adds richness, tenderness, and flavor without eggs, dairy, or added oil. Natural almond butter works well; stir it thoroughly before measuring if the oil has separated.
  • Coconut sugar. Coconut sugar adds gentle sweetness and a subtle caramel note. Other granular or liquid sweetener options are included below.
  • Vanilla extract. Vanilla rounds out the cinnamon and complements the nutty, toasted flavor of the flaxseed.

How to Make Vegan Flaxseed Muffins

Note that the complete directions are also in the recipe card below.

  • 1. Prepare the pan. Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Line 6 cups of a standard muffin tin with silicone, paper, or foil liners. Silicone liners give the most reliable nonstick release.
  • 2. Grind the flaxseed meal. Place the flaxseed meal—or whole flaxseeds, if using—in a blender. Process for 30 to 60 seconds, or longer for whole seeds, until it has the fine texture of flour.
  • 3. Combine the dry ingredients. In a medium bowl, whisk the flaxseed flour, whole psyllium husks, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt until evenly combined. Breaking up any small clumps now helps the finished muffins bake evenly.
  • 4. Mix the wet ingredients. In a large mixing bowl, whisk the boiling water, almond butter, coconut sugar, and vanilla until blended and smooth.
  • 5. Make the dough. Add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture and stir until completely combined. The mixture should be a thick, moist dough, not a pourable muffin batter. Do not add extra water.
  • 6. Fill the muffin cups. Divide the dough evenly among the 6 prepared cups and smooth the tops. For especially smooth, rounded tops, roll each portion gently into a ball before placing it in its liner.
  • 7. Bake and cool. Bake in the center of the preheated oven for 25 minutes. The cinnamon flax muffins will have a modest rise; they are ready when the tops look dry and feel firm to the touch. Transfer the muffins to a wire rack and let them cool completely. The crumb continues to set as the muffins cool, so do not rush this step.

Taste and Texture

I know you will love that these cinnamon flaxseed muffins taste warm and familiar, with fragrant vanilla and cinnamon and a gently toasted flavor from the flaxseed flour. The crumb is moist, tender, and dense (similar to a traditional bran muffin) and the muffins are exceptionally satisfying.

Because they are high-fiber vegan muffins, they are not light and cake-y, like bakery muffins. They are not intended to be. These are robust, keep-you-full muffins, 100%. Their hearty texture and balanced sweetness make them especially good for portable breakfasts or substantial snacks.

Storage for the Vegan Flaxseed Muffins

Store the cooled muffins in an airtight container at cool room temperature for 2 days, the refrigerator for 1 week or the freezer for up to 6 months.

Tips for Success

Weigh the ingredients when possible

For the most consistent results, weigh the ingredients. Use the metric toggle in the recipe card for gram measurements. This is especially important for the flaxseed and psyllium.

Grind the flaxseed meal into flour

Even packaged flaxseed meal needs to be processed into a finer flour. This improves the texture and helps the muffins bind properly. Do not skip this step.

Identify your psyllium before measuring

Whole psyllium husks and psyllium powder have very different volumes. Use 30 grams of either one. That is about 6 tablespoons of whole husks or about 6 teaspoons of powder.

Use silicone liners for the easiest release

These egg-free, oil-free muffins can cling to paper. Silicone muffin liners provide the cleanest, most dependable release.

Do not add more water

The mixture should look like a thick, moist dough, not conventional muffin batter. Extra liquid can produce muffins with large air pockets and gooey centers.

Do not use defatted flax flour

This recipe was developed with full-fat flaxseed meal or whole flaxseeds. Defatted flax flour behaves differently. Check the nutrition label: a product listing zero fat or calories from fat is likely defatted and may not produce the same result.

Use baking powder, not baking soda

Baking powder and baking soda work differently. Baking soda is much stronger than baking powder and is not a substitute in this recipe

Cool the muffins completely

The interior sets as it cools. Cutting into a hot muffin may make a properly baked center appear too soft.

Ingredient Substitutions

Flaxseed meal

Replace the flaxseed meal with an equal weight—156 grams—of whole flaxseeds. This is just over 15 tablespoons of seeds (a tablespoon under 1 cup). Grind the seeds in a blender until they become a very fine flour.

Coconut sugar

Replace the coconut sugar with an equal amount of another granular sweetener, such as brown sugar, cane sugar, or date sugar. A cup-for-cup sugar-free baking sweetener can also be used.

For a liquid sweetener such as maple syrup, agave nectar, or date syrup, use the same amount of sweetener and reduce the boiling water from 2/3 cup to 1/2 cup.

Almond butter

Use an equal amount of another nut or seed butter, such as cashew butter, peanut butter, tahini, sunflower seed butter, or pumpkin seed butter. The flavor will vary with your choice.

Psyllium husks

Replace the whole psyllium husks with an equal weight—30 grams—of psyllium powder. By volume, this is about 6 teaspoons of powder rather than 6 tablespoons of whole husks.

Water

Replace the boiling water with an equal amount of very hot nondairy milk, such as almond milk, coconut milk beverage or soy milk

Flavor Variations and Add-Ins

Fold up to 1/2 cup of one of the following into the finished dough:

  • Chopped dried fruit, such as cranberries, raisins, or apricots
  • Dark chocolate chips or chopped dark chocolate
  • Chopped raw or toasted nuts, such as walnuts, pecans, or almonds
  • Seeds, such as sunflower seeds or green pumpkin seeds (pepitas)
  • For an almond variation, replace the vanilla extract with 1 teaspoon of almond extract.
  • Vary the spices. Replace the cinnamon with 1 to 1 1/2 teaspoons of ground cardamom, pumpkin pie spice, allspice or ginger.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these flaxseed muffins grain-free?

Yes. Flaxseed is a seed, and this recipe does not contain wheat, oats, rice, or any other grain. Flaxseed meal is the only flour.

Are these muffins gluten-free?

Yes, the recipe ingredients are naturally gluten-free. If you need to avoid cross-contact, use products labeled or certified gluten-free, including the baking powder, and check every packaged ingredient.

Are these muffins oil-free?

They contain no added oil. Almond butter and full-fat flaxseed naturally contain fat, which contributes flavor and tenderness.

Can you make flax muffins without eggs?

Yes. These muffins are completely egg-free. Finely ground flaxseed and psyllium provide the binding and structure normally supplied by eggs and flour.

Can I make keto-friendly flax muffins?

For a lower-sugar variation, replace the coconut sugar with a cup-for-cup keto-friendly granulated baking sweetener. Choose an unsweetened nut or seed butter and check its nutrition label, since carbohydrate counts vary by product. Keep in mind that substitutions can change the muffins’ flavor and texture.

Can I use flax eggs in this recipe?

No separate flax eggs are needed. Flaxseed meal is already the primary ingredient and binder; use the boiling water exactly as directed.

Why is my muffin mixture so thick?

The muffin mixture is supposed to be thick. This recipe makes a thick, moist dough rather than a pourable batter. The low-moisture dough is essential for the success of these muffins. Too much liquid will cause a high rise bubble with a gooey bottom.

Why did my muffins turn out gooey inside?

The most likely causes are adding extra liquid, measuring the psyllium incorrectly, not grinding the flaxseed meal finely enough, or cutting the muffins before they cooled completely. Weigh the ingredients, confirm whether you have husks or powder, and follow the cooling step.

Happy baking!

Vegan Flaxseed Muffins

Moist, tender, and satisfyingly dense, these cinnamon flaxseed muffins use flaxseed meal as the only flour. They are vegan, gluten-free, grain-free, and oil-free, with 8 grams of protein and 12 grams of fiber per muffin. Grind the flaxseed meal into a fine flour and measure the psyllium carefully for the best texture.
Servings: 6 muffins

Equipment

  • 1 standard size muffin tin (12-count) or a 6-count muffin tin
  • silicone muffin tin liners

Ingredients 

  • 1 1/2 cups flaxseed meal, (see notes for whole flaxseed
  • 6 tablespoons whole psyllium husks
  • 2 1/2 teaspoons baking powder, (certified gluten-free, as needed)
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 2/3 cup boiling water
  • 1/3 cup almond butter, (see ingredients substitutions section)
  • 1/4 cup coconut sugar, (see ingredients substitutions section)
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
US Customary – Metric
Prevent your screen from going dark

Instructions 

  • Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Line 6 cups of a standard muffin tin with silicone, paper, or foil liners. Silicone liners give the most reliable nonstick release.
  • Place the flaxseed meal—or whole flaxseeds, if using—in a blender. Process for 30 to 60 seconds, or longer for whole seeds, until it has the fine texture of flour.
  • In a medium bowl, whisk the flaxseed flour, whole psyllium husks, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt until evenly combined. Breaking up any small clumps now helps the finished muffins bake evenly.
  • In a large mixing bowl, whisk the boiling water, almond butter, coconut sugar, and vanilla until blended and smooth.
  • Add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture and stir until completely combined. The mixture should be a thick, moist dough, not a pourable muffin batter. Do not add extra water.
  • Divide the dough evenly among the 6 prepared cups and smooth the tops. For especially smooth, rounded tops, roll each portion gently into a ball before placing it in its liner.
  • Bake in the center of the preheated oven for 25 minutes. The muffins will have a modest rise; they are ready when the tops look dry and feel firm to the touch.
  • Transfer the muffins to a wire rack and let them cool completely. The crumb continues to set as the muffins cool, so do not rush this step.

Notes

Storage: Store the cooled muffins in an airtight container at cool room temperature for 2 days, the refrigerator for 1 week or the freezer for up to 6 months.
Ingredient Substitutions

  • Coconut sugar: Replace the coconut sugar with an equal amount of another granular sweetener, such as brown sugar, cane sugar, or date sugar. A cup-for-cup sugar-free baking sweetener can also be used. For a liquid sweetener such as maple syrup, agave nectar, or date syrup, use the same amount of sweetener and reduce the boiling water from 2/3 cup to 1/2 cup.
  • Almond butter: Use an equal amount of another nut or seed butter, such as cashew butter, peanut butter, tahini, sunflower seed butter, or pumpkin seed butter. The flavor will vary with your choice.
  • Whole Psyllium Husks: Replace the whole psyllium husks with an equal weight—30 grams—of psyllium powder. By volume, this is about 6 teaspoons of powder rather than 6 tablespoons of whole husks.
