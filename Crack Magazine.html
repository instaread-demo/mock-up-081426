Biennale Musica 2026 announces full programme

The Tallis Scholars
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 2:07
Instaread

Tickets are now on sale for the 70th edition of La Biennale di Venezia’s International Festival of Contemporary Music.

The 70th edition of Biennale Musica will take place in Venice from 10 to 24 October under the theme A Child of Sound.

The 2026 edition is the second helmed by artistic director Caterina Barbieri, the Berlin-based composer and electronic musician. This year’s programme continues her approach of combining historical and contemporary music while placing greater emphasis on experimental practices and non-Western musical traditions.

Among the featured performances, the Parco della Musica Contemporanea Ensemble brings together Musica per una fine by Ennio Morricone with Johann Sebastian Bach and Sarah Davachi.

Alif Hilal, formerly known as Lyra Pramuk, will collaborate with Iranian percussionist Mohammad Reza Mortazavi and Egyptian multi-instrumentalist Islam El-Ghazouly on the new project In the Threshold of Your Love.

The Tallis Scholars will perform under the vaults of Saint Mark’s Basilica, with a programme spanning music from Hildegard von Bingen and Gregorio Allegri to Arvo Pärt and Kara-Lis Coverdale, while composer Clarissa Connelly will present Canons – a work that combines Nordic folk traditions, medieval music and digital sound elements.

Other highlights include Walter Zanetti’s Cantos Yoruba de Cuba, works by Cuban composer Leo Brouwer, a guitar-focused event by Belgian ensemble Zwerm featuring new commissions from ML Buch and Gigi Masin, a performance from Moroccan musician Asmaâ Hamzaoui with her group Bnat Timbouktou, and a performance from Japanese experimental musician Keiji Haino.

Artists including DJ Travella, Jay Mitta and Pili Pili will represent Tanzania’s Singeli scene. Lisbon label Príncipe will showcase artists including Nídia, DJ Firmeza and Helviofox.

The wider line-up includes Polygonia, DJ Nobu, Carrier, Loidis, Livwutang, Mechatok, 100_ve and DJ Anderson do Paraíso, as well as the multisensory performance As Nature by Kenyan sound artist KMRU with Nick Verstand and Mareike Bode.

Many events will be staged in the Sale d’Armi at the Arsenale, where the festival’s Origine programme will host concerts, listening sessions, screenings and talks curated in part by Masaru Hatanaka of Tokyo’s Nightingale listening bar.

    Blue Note Jazz Club to open first UK venue in London

    The Covent Garden venue opens this September, with a debut programme featuring Erykah Badu, Nubya Garcia, Yussef Dayes and more.

    Blue Note Jazz Club is opening its first UK venue in London’s West End on 23 September.

    Located on St Martin’s Lane in Covent Garden, Blue Note London will present a programme of British and international artists spanning jazz and related genres. The venue follows the model of the club’s locations in New York, Los Angeles, Milan and other cities, combining legendary names with emerging talent.

    The first season will feature performances and residencies from artists including Erykah Badu, Nubya Garcia, Yussef Dayes, KOKOROKO, Hak Baker and Jamie Cullum, with more artists to be announced.

    Five-time GRAMMY Award winner Robert Glasper will open the venue with performances on 23 and 24 September before returning for a further seven shows in November. Glasper will also serve as a creative ambassador for Blue Note London, helping shape the venue’s artistic direction.

    “Starting this new club is about creating a space where jazz, R&B, hip-hop, and everything in between can meet, evolve, and surprise people,” Glasper says. “I’m excited to build something in London that feels intimate, fearless, and alive, a place where artists can take chances and audiences can experience the music up close.”

    Designed by Julien Legeard of Legeard Studio, Blue Note London will feature two performance spaces. The main room will hold 250 people and host two shows each evening at 7pm and 9.30pm. A second 100-capacity space, B-Side, is due to open later in 2026 and will focus on emerging British artists.

    The venue will be open until 1am from Monday to Saturday and until midnight on Sundays. Alongside its bar and cocktail menu, Blue Note London will also serve dinner throughout the week.

    Tsion Bensusan, Co-Owner of Blue Note Entertainment Group, says: “For more than four decades, Blue Note has been dedicated to creating intimate spaces where artists and audiences connect through unforgettable live performances. Our hope is that Blue Note London becomes more than a venue, it becomes a home for musicians, a gathering place for the community, and an integral part of London’s cultural landscape.”

    “We’ll be welcoming some of the greatest jazz artists worldwide whilst shining a light on our exceptional emerging homegrown UK jazz scene, which has been on fire in recent years,” adds Nick Lewis, Director of Music & Programming at Blue Note London. “Audiences will experience the very best of jazz and adjacent genres, nightly in the heart of London.”

    Tickets go on general sale at 10am on Friday, 14 August, the Blue Note Membership presale opens at 10 am on Wednesday, 12 August, and venue presale begins at 10am on Thursday, 13 August. Sign up here.
