How to Play Fall Bingo

If your kids already know how to play bingo, they can jump right into this game. If not, the rules are easy to learn.

  1. Give each player one card and a set of bingo tokens.
  2. Have everyone cover the FREE space in the center of the board.
  3. Cut the calling cards apart, shuffle them, and place them face down in a pile or a bowl.
  4. Choose one person to be the bingo caller.
  5. The caller draws one picture at a time and announces what it is.
  6. Players look for the matching picture on their bingo card.
  7. If they have the picture, they place a token on that square.
  8. Continue drawing cards until someone covers five spaces in a row horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.
  9. The first player to complete a row calls out “Bingo!”
  10. Check their winning spaces against the calling cards that have already been drawn.

When playing with preschoolers or other younger children, hold up each calling card so they can see the picture while you name it. This makes it much easier for them to find the matching image on their board.

For toddlers and preschoolers, choose larger markers that are appropriate for their age and supervise any activity involving small pieces.

Fun Ways to Play Fall Bingo

Traditional bingo isn’t the only way to use these cards. Changing the winning pattern is an easy way to keep kids interested through several rounds.

Traditional Bingo – Cover five pictures in a row horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

Four Corners – The first person to cover all four corners of their card wins.

X Bingo – Players must complete both diagonal rows so that the covered squares form a large X across the board.

Picture Hunt Bingo – Instead of immediately naming the picture on the calling card, hold it up and let the kids identify the item first. This is an easy way to add a little fall vocabulary practice to the game.

Team Bingo – Pair kids together and have them share a card. This works especially well for classroom parties or other groups with more than 10 children.

Fall Bingo for Preschoolers and Kindergarten

One of the best things about this game is that the cards rely on pictures instead of words. That makes the game especially easy for preschoolers, kindergarteners, and beginning readers.

While they’re having fun, younger children can also practice several basic skills, including:

  • Matching pictures
  • Listening carefully
  • Following directions
  • Taking turns
  • Visual recognition
  • Learning fall vocabulary
  • Concentration
  • Fine motor skills from placing bingo markers

For preschoolers, say each picture’s name clearly as you hold up the calling card. You can also ask simple questions, such as “Who can find the pumpkin?” or “What color is this apple?” to add a little extra learning to the activity.

How to Play With More Than 10 Kids

There are 10 different bingo boards in this printable, but you can still use the game with a larger classroom or party group.

One easy option is to divide the children into pairs and give each pair a card. This can be especially helpful when younger and older kids are playing together.

In the classroom, you can also use the game as a small-group center with up to 10 students at a time. Rotate groups throughout the day so everyone gets a chance to play.

Another option is to print duplicate boards. Just keep in mind that children with matching boards may get bingo at the same time.

You can also play several quick rounds and have children trade cards after each game.

There are 10 different cards in the printable set. Each one has a unique arrangement of the seasonal pictures.

Are calling cards included?

Yes! The printable includes a full page of individual picture calling cards that can be cut apart before playing.

Are bingo markers included?

Yes. There is also a separate page of tokens to cut out, so you don’t need to provide your own bingo markers unless you want to.

What ages can play this game?
